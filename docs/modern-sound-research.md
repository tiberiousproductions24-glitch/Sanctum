# Modern church / gospel production sound — research notes (Nov 2026)

Purpose: functional and sonic reference for building **original synthesized** Sanctum banks (v0.3.1).
No audio was downloaded, ripped or sampled from YouTube or anywhere else. No specific copyrighted
recording was used as a source. These notes describe genre conventions, synthesis recipes and mix
targets that are common knowledge among producers.

Researched 2026-11-05 (America/Los_Angeles) with web search; sources listed per section.

## 1. Gospel trap / Christian trap ("Trap Sanctuary")
- Tempo 130–150 BPM felt half-time (65–75), clap/snare on beat 3 of the half-time bar, ghost notes and
  rimshots for bounce. Minor keys with gospel extensions (add9, 11, 13); organ, choir and piano textures
  over precisely programmed drums — the contrast is the signature.
- **808**: tuned to the key and following the chord roots; alternate long notes with quick glides.
  Keep the sub (< 80–100 Hz) clean and distort / saturate only the mid band (100–800 Hz) in parallel so
  the 808 reads on phone speakers. Layer a short click/transient on top for attack.
- **Hats**: 1/16 base with 1/32 and triplet rolls, velocity and pitch variation, occasional open hat on
  the upbeat. **Kicks**: short, punchy, clicky top.
- Mix: high-pass everything that isn't kick/808, leave headroom, parallel compression on drums.
- Sources: Prosound Sonics "How to Make Gospel Trap Beats"
  (https://www.prosoundsonics.com/blogs/prosound-sonics/how-to-make-gospel-trap-beats-chords-melodies-808s);
  Melodigging "Christian Trap" (https://www.melodigging.com/genre/christian-trap);
  Unison "Transforming your 808s" (https://unison.audio/transforming-your-808s/);
  iZotope "How to mix 808s" (https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/how-to-mix-808s);
  Sage Audio "How to mix trap" (https://www.sageaudio.com/articles/how-to-mix-trap).

## 2. Drill / gospel drill ("Drill Pulpit")
- 138–145 BPM, half-time feel. Hats on a **3-3-2** grouping over 16ths (from grime), with gaps,
  1/32 rolls and triplets; slightly irregular is better.
- Snare on beat 3, **displaced later** in the second bar, ghost snares; rim/perc fills before the snare.
  Kick is sparse and off the downbeat.
- **Sliding 808** is the giveaway: long sustained notes with portamento, often jumping a 5th or an octave;
  layered "main / reverb / electrocuted (distorted)" 808s. Dark piano, strings or choir on top.
- Sources: Songen "How to make drill drums" (https://songen.app/blog/how-to-make-drill-drums/);
  The Sonic Secrets "How to make a drill beat" (https://thesonicsecrets.com/production/how-to-make-a-drill-beat/);
  Attack Magazine "UK Drill" (https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/uk-drill/);
  Navid Hamidi "Key elements of a classic UK drill beat" (https://navidhamidi.com/key-elements-of-a-classic-uk-drill-beat/);
  BandLab blog (https://blog.bandlab.com/how-to-make-a-uk-drill-beat-on-bandlab/).

## 3. Amapiano / Afro-gospel praise ("Amapiano Praise")
- ~110–115 BPM (112 is typical), soft four-on-the-floor kick, constant swung 16th shakers with accents,
  offbeat open hats, syncopated rims / congas / woodblocks; 54–58 % swing.
- **Log drum** = synthesized pitched bass hit (originally an FM preset): low sine/triangle carrier,
  one modulator 1–2 octaves up with a fast-decaying FM index (the metallic "pock"), pitch envelope
  +3…+8 st falling in 25–80 ms, zero-attack amp, 150–300 ms decay, saturation so it reads on phones,
  LP ~1.5 kHz. Written as bursts of 3–5 notes per bar, A0–A1 range, written *around* the kick
  (do not sidechain it).
- Sources: Music Production Wiki "How to recreate the amapiano log drum"
  (https://musicproductionwiki.com/articles/how-to-recreate-the-amapiano-log-drum);
  Ableton 12 Sound Design Atlas — Afro House & Amapiano (https://ableton.onefootstep.us/genres/afro-house-amapiano.html);
  The Sonic Secrets amapiano drum pattern (https://thesonicsecrets.com/production/drum-patterns/amapiano/);
  Roland "Production hacks: creating amapiano tracks" (https://articles.roland.com/production-hacks-creating-amapiano-tracks/).

## 4. Neo-soul / "Sunday service" keys ("Neo-Soul Sunday")
- Electric-piano (Rhodes-type) voicings: small, tight 3–5 note shapes; major chords get 9 + 13,
  minor chords 9 + 11, dominants often replaced by **13sus**; cluster voicings (9-3-11);
  a **common tone on top** across the progression; cyclic progressions that move by small steps
  (e.g. ii11 → vi9 → IVmaj13 → iii11 style motion) instead of hard V–I. Grace-note slides from a half-step below.
- Drums: dry and close, lazy/behind-the-beat swing, brushes or soft snare, then bus tape saturation,
  LP ~10–12 kHz, very light wow/flutter and low-level vinyl crackle.
- Sources: Piano With Jonny "4 steps to neo soul chords" (https://pianowithjonny.com/piano-lessons/4-steps-to-play-neo-soul-chords-on-piano/);
  PianoGroove "Neo soul harmony" (https://www.pianogroove.com/live-seminars/neo-soul-jazz-harmony/);
  Scott Healy / Keyboard "The Rhodes less traveled" (https://www.bluedogmusic.com/rhodes-less-traveled-4023);
  UJAM "How to make neo soul drums" (https://www.ujam.com/tutorials/how-to-make-neo-soul-drums/);
  VST Shop "Lo-fi tape drums" (https://vstshop.com/knowledge/lofi-tape-drums-guide/).

## 5. Hammond organ, Leslie growl, shout music "chops"
- Tonewheel = additive sines at 16', 5⅓', 8', 4', 2⅔', 2', 1⅗', 1⅓', 1' (0.5, 1.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 × f).
  Gospel registrations: **888 000 000** (shout), **808 808 008**, **808 605 004** (full choir), full 888 888 888 for climaxes.
  Fast Leslie: horn (~6.7 Hz) = Doppler FM + AM, drum (~5.8 Hz) = mostly AM. Growl = preamp overdrive
  (soft clip) before the Leslie; key click on attack.
- Shout music: 120–190 BPM praise break, four-on-the-floor kick, heavy backbeat, syncopated hats,
  fast fills landing on the next downbeat; organ walking runs and chordal stabs.
- Sources: Hammondentusiasterne drawbar settings (http://hammondentusiasterne.dk/?page_id=809);
  Pekonen et al., "Computationally efficient Hammond organ synthesis", DAFx-11
  (https://research.spa.aalto.fi/publications/papers/dafx11-hammond/Pekonen2011DAFx.pdf);
  Sound On Sound "Synthesizing Hammond organ effects" (https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/synthesizing-hammond-organ-effects);
  HammondWiki "How to get more distortion" (https://www.dairiki.org/HammondWiki/HowToGetMoreDistortion);
  Wikipedia "Shout (Black gospel music)" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shout_(Black_gospel_music));
  Rhythm Dictionary "Gospel shout" (https://rhythmdictionary.com/p/gospel-shout).

## 6. Synths, cinematic worship FX, synthesized choir
- **Supersaw pad**: 7–9 detuned saws, wide stereo, gentle LP, 20–80 ms+ attack, long release, slow LFO, reverb.
- **Reese bass**: two saws detuned ~±0.3 st (beating), mono, LP ~650 Hz.
- **Transitions**: 2–4 bar riser (pitch + filter + noise up, ending exactly on the downbeat), impact =
  low kick + noise crack + sub thud (< 120 Hz mono), reverse swell into the cut, tape stop (pitch and
  speed fall together), 0.1–0.3 s of silence before the reveal.
- **Formant "ooh/aah" choir (no real voices)**: sawtooth/glottal source through parallel band-pass
  formants. Sung /u/ "ooh" ≈ F1 350–400, F2 600–750, F3 2400 Hz; /a/ "aah" ≈ F1 600, F2 1040, F3 2250 Hz.
  Choir thickness = several detuned voices with vibrato (~5 Hz) and breath noise.
- Sources: LiveKeyboardist "Making a super saw patch" (https://livekeyboardist.com/supersaw/);
  Native Instruments "Reese bass" (https://www.native-instruments.com/blogs/blog/reese-bass);
  Ableton Sound Design Atlas — FX, risers & impacts (https://ableton.onefootstep.us/sounds/fx-risers-impacts.html);
  Onomo "Transition FX guide" (https://www.onomo.io/blog/transition-fx-guide);
  Ableton blog "High-impact sounds for movies and trailers" (https://www.ableton.com/en/blog/learn-how-to-make-high-impact-sounds-for-movies-and-trailers/);
  KTH "Formant frequencies in choir singers" (https://www.speech.kth.se/qpsr/1987/1987_28_4_043-055.pdf);
  U. Manchester three-formant vowel synthesiser (https://personalpages.manchester.ac.uk/staff/richard.baker/WebDemonstrations/Speech/vowel.html).
